About Me

Ph.D. candidate in ICT at Sapienza University of Rome — building simple, deployable scheduling policies for large-scale computing and LLM inference.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Information and Communication Technology at Sapienza University of Rome, where I have been working since November 2023 under the supervision of Prof. Andrea Baiocchi, with an expected defense in January 2027. My research focuses on performance evaluation and resource allocation in large-scale computing systems — the question of how to keep big clusters busy without keeping users waiting.

At its core, my work develops practical dispatching and scheduling policies for heterogeneous, multi-resource clusters with CPU/GPU and memory constraints, evaluated against production traces from Google Borg and Alibaba. A recurring theme is that near-optimal performance can be reached with simple, deployable policies that do not require job-size information — a property that matters enormously in real data centers, where such information is often unavailable or unreliable. To study this, I combine queueing-theoretic models, trace-driven workload analysis, discrete-event simulation, and hands-on benchmarking.

I have also extended this line of work to AI infrastructure: benchmarks for LLM inference on multi-GPU systems spanning 8B- to 70B-parameter models, covering CPU–GPU offloading, preemption, and KV-cache migration, as well as schedulers for serving multiple LLMs concurrently. More broadly, I work on 5G NR V2X sidelink and vehicular networks toward 6G — studying Age of Information, persistent scheduling, and safety-critical delay metrics for connected vehicles.

My research is deeply collaborative. I am a visiting researcher at the IMDEA Networks Institute in Madrid (February–July 2026) under Prof. Marco Ajmone Marsan, and I have worked with groups at KTH, the University of Torino, LIST, and KIT. At Sapienza I also mentor M.Sc. Data Science students — two have graduated so far, and one is ongoing on stochastic optimization for energy efficiency.

Before my Ph.D., I spent six years in industry as Lead Ad Operations Analyst at Compulse (formerly ZypTv), where I built automated reporting pipelines and cross-platform ad analytics. That experience taught me how to wire data and services together into systems that actually work — and how to deliver under pressure. I hold a B.Sc. in Industrial Engineering and an M.Sc. in Data Science, with a final grade of 110/110.
