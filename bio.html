Bio

Ph.D. candidate in ICT at Sapienza University of Rome — keeping giant computing clusters busy without keeping anyone waiting.

My research question is deceptively simple: how do you keep a giant cluster busy without keeping anyone waiting? Behind that question sits the machinery of modern computing — data centers, HPC systems, and increasingly the GPU fleets that serve AI models. I design scheduling policies that are simple enough to deploy in production, yet strong enough to reach near-optimal performance.

What excites me is the gap between theory and practice. The textbook answer to optimal scheduling usually assumes you know each job's size in advance — information real data centers simply don't have. So I work on policies that are deployable as-is, and I test them the hard way: against production traces from Google Borg and Alibaba, with queueing models, simulation, and hands-on benchmarking.

Lately this line of work has extended to AI infrastructure — how several large language models share a single GPU server, when it pays to preempt or offload work, and what that costs in energy and delay. I also study connected vehicles: how quickly safety-critical information can travel between cars on the road, and what “fresh enough” really means for 6G.

The path here has not been a straight line: an engineering degree in Izmir, six years in San Francisco building ad-analytics pipelines at Compulse, and then Rome for the Ph.D. That industry stint still shapes how I do research: I care about systems that actually work under pressure, not just models that look good on paper. Along the way I have mentored M.Sc. students — two already graduated — served on conference program committees, and won a few hackathons (including first prize at HackUPC).

My work is deeply collaborative: I am currently a visiting researcher at the IMDEA Networks Institute in Madrid under Prof. Marco Ajmone Marsan, and I have worked with groups at KTH, the University of Torino, LIST, and KIT. Before my Ph.D. I hold an M.Sc. in Data Science (110/110) and a B.Sc. in Industrial Engineering — which is where the love of queues and optimization began.
