About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Information and Communication Technology at Sapienza University of Rome (expected defense January 2027), working on performance evaluation and resource allocation in large-scale computing systems. My research develops practical dispatching and scheduling policies for heterogeneous, multi-resource clusters — CPU/GPU and memory — and evaluates them against production traces from Google Borg and Alibaba. A recurring theme is reaching near-optimal performance with simple, deployable policies that do not rely on job-size information, combining queueing-theoretic models with trace-driven analysis, discrete-event simulation, and benchmarking.

I have extended this line of work to AI infrastructure — benchmarking CPU–GPU memory offloading, preemption, and KV-cache migration for LLM inference serving across multi-GPU systems, and co-designing schedulers for multi-model LLM inference. I also contribute to research on resource scheduling and delay metrics in 5G NR V2X sidelink and vehicular networks toward 6G. I collaborate with IMDEA Networks Institute (Madrid), KTH Royal Institute of Technology, the University of Torino, the Luxembourg Institute of Science and Technology (LIST), and the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), and I mentor M.Sc. students in Data Science at Sapienza. Beyond academia, I bring six years of industry experience in ad-operations analytics from Compulse (formerly ZypTv), where I progressed from Operations Analyst to Lead Ad Operations Analyst.

Research Interests

Computational Resource Allocation

Analyzing multiserver jobs in data centers and HPC clusters using queueing theory, grounded in real workloads (Google Borg, Alibaba traces).

Multiobjective Optimization

Dispatching and scheduling multiserver jobs with a focus on the stochastic trade-off between energy efficiency and response time.

HPC Performance Prediction

Machine-learning models for predicting computational job execution times to improve scheduling decisions.

LLM Inference Infrastructure

Schedulers for multi-model LLM inference: CPU–GPU offloading, preemption, KV-cache migration, and multi-model serving across multi-GPU systems.

Vehicular Communication

Resource management and optimization in V2X networks — Age of Information, persistent scheduling, and safety-critical delay metrics toward 6G.
